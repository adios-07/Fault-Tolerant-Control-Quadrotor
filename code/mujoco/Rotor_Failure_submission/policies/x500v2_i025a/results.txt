x500v2_i025a: passive fault-tolerant low-level policy, successor of i023b.
Same interface: 27-in / 4-out ONNX (opset 17, IR 8), same pftc_ref.py (unchanged), shadow mode before the handover,
0.27 N floor. ONNX vs JAX on 12,000 closed-loop observations: max difference 1.1e-6.
Training: fine-tune of i023b, 150M steps, joint PPO, all i023b settings (onboard estimator model, airframe errors,
prop loss, flight-phase starts, PX4 stand-in 0-120 ms) plus partial loss and the symmetric structure below.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE i023b
1. Partial loss: 30 % of training faults leave the failed rotor at 30-80 % thrust (thrust and drag torque scaled
   together). Below 50 % the policy flies it like a complete loss (yaw spin allowed, PX4 stand-in before the
   handover); from 50 % up it holds yaw like a healthy drone. i023b had never been trained on it (5-7/20 crashes at
   50-65 %); i025a: 0 crashes at 30/50/65/80 % in hover and in all 5 phases.
2. Exactly symmetric policy: every motor fault now gives exactly the same result (mirror / rotation image).
   i023b was not: its fault copies shared one network, but the selector that picks the copy and the healthy expert
   were free networks (action difference up to 1.7 between mirrored views of the same moment). Now the selector
   scores "motor i failed" only on the view in which motor i looks like motor 0, and the healthy expert is averaged
   over the 4 symmetries. Measured equivariance error: 2.4e-7 (float rounding). The ONNX graph has the same structure.
